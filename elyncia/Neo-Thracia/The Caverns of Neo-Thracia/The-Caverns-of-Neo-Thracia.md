## Forward

*The Caverns of Thracia* stands first among the dungeons and adventure
scenarios that **Jennell Jaquays** designed and illustrated in their
entirety, published in 1979 by Judges Guild for the original *Dungeons &
Dragons®* rules of TSR. That work forms the origin-stratum beneath
everything that follows — a base layer this telling honors, cites, and
builds upon, never overwrites.

*Flying Triremes & Laser Swords* re-keys those caverns onto the
**Synthetic Dream Machine** by **Luka Rejec**. Where the
original named its monsters, treasures, and rooms in one ruleset's
grammar, this telling folds the whole into Elyncia, where the fall of Thracia becomes the fall of the Neo-Thracian Empire.

Each referee will reshape this material to fit the table they moderate.
Treat every statline, faction, and corridor below as a starting bearing,
not a fixed law. This adaptation claims no endorsement by TSR, Wizards of the Coast, Judges Guild, or Luka Rejec.

— Amorphous Dreams Cabal, YOLD 5492

### Designer’s Comments

Jennell Jaquays designed the Caverns of Thracia to accommodate adventurers
of the first tier and higher. Some segments play as a pushover for low-tier
groups, while one or two areas send higher-tier characters ducking for
cover when run correctly. As you read through this scenario — and Jennell
recommends reading it through before commencing play — you may notice the
designer’s voice turning informal at times. That informality serves on
purpose, since this adventure never reached for snob appeal, nor for
standing as the last word in gaming aids. The adventure now passes to your
table. Jennell set down possibilities and probabilities, with a few remarks
to go with them; it now falls to you to breathe life into the denizens of
this scenario. No high-power artifacts wait in these caverns, and only one
creature wields very high-power sorcery. Jennell surmised that even a
company of pure fighters could survive this adventure — though they would
miss some of the historical aspects and clues — and haul out a goodly sum
of loot. Treasure stays purposefully rare (though a few rather nice pieces
wait), and the monsters that know of one another in this dungeon usually
work together. Only two groups oppose one another here — the remnant Imperial scions and the
beast folk — and since they rarely frequent the same areas, little passes
between them beyond constant raiding.

### Starting the Adventure

Begin any adventure in a place the characters already know. Drop a few hints and let the players take it from there — asking the locals pertinent questions, poking their noses where noses go, the one habit every adventurer keeps. To speed things along, the referee may draw on the section titled *The Taverns of Neo-Thracia* to assign knowledge to the characters at random.

### Notes for the Referee

*After Jennell Jaquays' original notes (Judges Guild, 1979), re-keyed to Elyncia and the Synthetic Dream Machine. Jennell set the bearings; this telling follows them.*

**A) Location stays unpinned.** Jennell fixed these caverns to no single place on any published world — "it can be, but it is not." This telling keeps that freedom and makes it load-bearing: the Caverns lie *lost inside* the Neo-Thracian Nexus, their coordinate undetermined until the table pins it through play (see *The Taverns of Neo-Thracia*). Jennell's own suggestions translate cleanly — a misted offshore island once joined to the mainland by a drowned landbridge (the strange exposed-littoral coastlines); a ruined city at the edge of a subtropical forest (the karst-and-jungle marches of inland Thracia); a ruined city in forsaken hills or under a mountain's shoulder (the highland approaches above Lake Stentoris and further inland). Pick the bearing your table will sail toward.

**B) Room numbers run consecutive.** Jennell numbered the rooms straight through rather than restarting at each level — so the sacrificial altar of the Dark One reads as Room 50, not "Level 2, Room 23." This telling keeps her convention; a single index spans all four levels.

**C) Re-key freely.** Strengthen a room, soften another, swap contents that do not fit the world you moderate. Treat every statline and corridor below as a starting bearing, never a fixed law.

**D) Some creatures answer to no roster.** Jennell built several beasts specifically for these caverns, official to no rulebook — local color to unsettle the players who memorized the system. This telling keeps that habit: where a creature carries no SDM or FTLS entry, the unknown does its work, and nothing rattles a rules-reader like a thing the rules never named.

**E) Wandering checks run by level.** Each level that carries a random-encounter chart carries an Attention clock starting at 3+d6. Run the Random Encounter checks on the FTLS watch/turn cadence — every torch and turn spent rigging a rotted bridge or taxing a guardian-statue ticks the clock toward something arriving.

**F) The mythic underworld descent spans many sessions.** The Caverns run deep. Few tables clear it in one session, and Jennell counted on that.

**G) A death-practice survives here that may survive nowhere else.** The Caverns' regions hold a living worship of Thanatos — the death-god the locals now name only "The Dark One." Jennell wrote these priests as servants of death who command the dead the way a "good" priest turns them; this telling keeps her intent and re-grounds it in Elyncia.
Since Death fell silent and the soul-roads broke, what reads elsewhere as necromancy may read here as the *last working funeral-craft in the Nexus*. These practitioners take no fixed moral cast — death works through anything, so a follower need carry no particular alignment, and "evil" names the deeds a given priest commits, not the office itself. They speak and read New Thracian (never the very ancient tongue; see the history). Their craft runs on a death-practice Path: where a Free-Cities or Dominion adept would *banish* the restless, the Thracian death-priest *binds, calms, and directs* them — standing in for the office the silent god can no longer keep. Build their graded workings — raising the fallen, speaking with the dead, the killing word — from the FTLS Powers and ECM chapters along that Path, rather than from any foreign spell-list; the *Null Referee Resources* carry sample statlines. A priest holds bound dead by correct practice, not by force of will alone, and a soul of real weight may slip a poorly-kept binding when the rite lapses. Run the binding as orthopraxy: right rite, right name, right offering, right node. A delver who learns the working rite gains more than a weapon against the dead — that delver gains the ferryman's office, and can do the work the god sleeps through.

G\) In the Caverns of Thracia there exists a religion that may not exist elsewhere. These are the worshippers of Thanatos, the death god or as he is currently called “The Dark One”. Clerics in the service of this lawful evil god assume a lawful evil alignment although actual followers need not be of any particular alignment, since death can work through anything. The clerics in the service of this god speak and read in the Thracian language (but not the very ancient language, see history for details). They have the following special abilities: At 4th level they may animate dead as per the spell once per day; at 6th level they may speak with dead as per the spell once per day; at 7th level they may use finger of death once per day as per the spell. These spells are not in addition or extra to their normal number of clerical spells. In addition to the above, the cleric in the service of death may control a number of undead equal to those turned by a good cleric. A cleric may never control more undead than the indicated maximum number of beings that are turnable (2d6 or maximum of 12). A “D” on the chart indicates instant control. A number indicates that the number must be rolled over on 2d8; a “T” indicates control of 1d6 of the undead creatures. Once these creatures are under the control of a cleric of Thanatos, they will obey his or her command until they are destroyed. Undead of 5+ hit dice may make a saving roll once per day of equal to or over the controlling cleric’s wisdom +2.

H\) Have fun and enjoy yourselves. As real as gaming may seem at times, it’s still only a game. Direct any questions, complaints, hate mail, etc. to Jennell Jaquays c/o Judges Guild, 1165 N. University Ave., Decatur, IL 62526. If a question or topic is pertinent, the answer may be published in one or both of the magazines put out by Judges Guild.

# The Caverns of Neo-Thracia

### A Short History

This history is not provided to give the players a background on the history of the caverns. As far as the players are concerned, the caverns are a part of a lost civilization(s) that disappeared centuries before. Little is known of them except, possibly, its location and a few wild stories (see *The Taverns of Thracia*). Instead, this history is provided to give the referee a historical index of various events that occurred in the development of the cavern societies. It will also include an overview of the cultural development of the early Thracian civilizations.

Before man existed, or possibly before he discovered the benefits and curses of civilization, there existed the reptile race. The reptile race, while not large, had its own centers of civilization, including large cities and underground temple complexes. As man waxed, the reptile race waned, losing much of its arcane civilization and slowly degenerating or regressing into the race of beings that men call “lizard men”. A few pockets of more advanced lizard men exist, but they are slowly dwindling as the race dies out. Long before man discovered the caverns, the reptile race was beginning to slip backwards. The Immortal King, the ruler of the tattered remnants of lizard men who still know the ways of sorcery, was near death and had been dying for several centuries. With the coming of man to the area (a new source of food and slaves for the still powerful reptiles) came the death of the Immortal King. But his was not a true death. The dead body remained animated by the creature’s spirit. Even in death the Immortal King continued to rule as a lich, entombed in the ancient underground temple. Slowly the reptile race regressed to their present state, but not before they had watched the now plentiful humans discover the caverns and build their own temple and burial complexes. The lizard men at last sealed up the various entrances to their now crumbling great hall and slunk off into the fetid swamps, to return later as mercenaries for the beast kings.

Meanwhile, the humans had discovered the caverns, not realizing that the once feared reptile men (they were merely legendary now and the Immortal King was a bogeyman to scare children) still occupied hidden portions of the caverns. At this point the men were beginning to develop a civilization comparable to the early Mycenaean or Cretan civilizations of this world’s past. The art and architecture were almost identical with the above mentioned cultures. Eventually they sealed off several of their tomb complexes and began to concentrate on the civilization above ground. Half a millennium later they would construct the temples on second level and 200 years after that they would begin the golden age of Thracian culture.

The actual golden age began with the discovery of an immense cavern, deep below the natural caverns. It was not a natural chamber, but no note was taken of that. About this same time, the Thracian people had begun to use sub-human creatures for slaves, such as the gnolls, minotaurs and dog brothers, who began to carve the underground caverns into a huge garden and palace. During the reign of Argos II the vast work was completed and the underground area became a playground for the Thracian royalty. However, during the reign of Agamemnos, Argos’ grandson, the huge number of beast men that laired in the caverns revolted and took over the palace, wounding the king to the death and causing his gentry to flee. Most never made it to the surface. Those who did inspired either fear or fierceness into persons hearing the tale. The few bands of vengeful citizens that entered the caverns were never seen again.

Thus, did the beast men set themselves up in the underground domain, retaining control of the entire 3rd, 4th and 2nd levels while allowing the 1st level to go untouched. They chose an intelligent minotaur to rule them and then set down to decide what to do with their numerous captives.

Some 150 years later the worship of Thanatos, the death god, was outlawed and the few priests who served death were either tortured to death or were sealed in the chapel of Thanatos on the 1st level.

The first wave of invaders came soon after. On the Sesquicentennial anniversary (150) years of the overthrow of Thanatos, an invading army of barbarians (all non-Thracians are barbarians) was spurred on by descendants of the death worshippers to utterly destroy the city. The lax Thracian army was no match for frenzied barbarians and all but a fraction of the city’s population were put to the sword or carried off into slavery. During the destruction, the entrances to the caverns were destroyed and covered with rubble.

The few remaining Thracians attempted to make some kind of reconstruction but less than a quarter century later a second invasion of vicious invaders struck again. Those Thracians who fled into the encroaching jungle survived while the rest died. The few who did escape slipped back into savagery. 200 years later, they rediscovered the caverns and with them they found not only a new/old religion in the worship of the god known only through legend as “The Dark One”, but also the beast men.

The beast men by this time had taken over the entire cavern complex (those parts they could easily find without looking hard) and had opened up the caverns to the surface again. With the coming of humans again, they saw entirely new possibilities for food and pleasure. Soon after the caverns were reopened, the reptile race once again sought the location of their legends and in doing so allied themselves with the beast men.

The caverns are now the holy places both for the descendants of the Thracians and for the once powerful reptile race whose degenerated offspring still seek the reason for their existence.

### Some Notes About The Thracian Language

The very ancient Thracians had a written language. There are a few instances where it can be found in the caverns. However, between the time of the sealing of the Ancient Thracian tombs and the beginning of the golden age, the Thracian language changed drastically (something like the difference between classical Latin and modern Italian). A character who can read the Thracian language may pick out one word in ten of the Ancient Thracian language.

## The Taverns of Neo-Thracia (Rumors)
----------------------

You will hear it before you ever see the place. In the lake-towns and the fossil-road inns, in the reed-hush where the barge-folk drink, the Caverns live as talk --- scraps of legend, half a truth wearing the other half's coat. None of it arrives whole. A lost thing stays lost not by silence, but by a hundred tellings that no longer agree.

Use this section or leave it; the referee decides. It hands out **legends** as *data shards*, splinters of old knowing that drifted loose from wherever they started and picked up grit on the way. Scatter them among your NPCs, or, when the table wants momentum more than mystery, deal them straight to the players by the method below.

Each shard a character carries, they carry on their own (slip of paper or private character sheet note). Tell them they *know* this thing about the road ahead. Tell them they may keep it close, trade it, or lie about it as they please --- a shard withheld becomes its own kind of coin. And tell them nothing of its weight, because not every shard rings true. The false ones wear an **(F)** where only the referee can see it. The referee has no obligation to inform the player as to the veracity of this information.

## Adventurers' Guild Referee Intel (Rumors)

*Circulating among Ainos locals, river traders, hill-tribe contacts. 489 years of oral drift, deliberate misdirection, and true memory, indistinguishable by ear. (F) where strong suspicion sits. Confidence rates corroboration, not volume.*

**1.** (F) *The Beast Folk began as cursed Imperial citizens.* Surfaces only in imperial-descended houses. Erases the slaves who carved the palace; reframes a free kingdom as a fault to fix. The score marks the slant. Cruel joke the teller never hears: all human folk carry the fae braid, so the "pure" the curse would restore never existed. Repeat it to a beast-kin patrol and mark yourself remnant-aligned --- reaction-rolls follow.

**2.** *Someone in Ainos buys intact Thracian artifacts --- 200--2,000 cash, no questions.* Price keys to *pattern-intact*, not age: live inscription, working daemon-binding pays double. The "no questions" builds the market; the unnamed patron asks plenty elsewhere --- Delian cataloguer, Dominion cell, Amaranth claim-agent, referee's pick. Every sale feeds someone's map of the Caverns. First sale opens a door; third opens a watcher behind it.

**3.** *Beware the Yellow Death.* Two decades of sources, none will specify. Fits spore-bloom, sulfur-tinged necro-fog, orichalcum gone jaundiced from feeding wrong, and one hireling's yellow poncho. Ask "yellow *what*?" and earn a look, not an answer --- locally everyone buried someone. Keep the referent open; let dread assign it.

**4.** *Don't trust the trees.* Triple-true. Ambushers in the canopy. Grove-fae below carrying a desire-steering charm that bends gnolls as easily as delvers. And the grove *listens* --- word crosses it by nightfall. The seam underneath: the fae grieve sisters felled for boiler-fuel, and water, mourning, and one honest word about the boilers buys allies no army could.

**5.** *The hill-tribes feed the dark with human sacrifice.* True in act, open in meaning. Death silent 489 years, soul-roads luring spirits into eddies --- a rite worked at the dreaming god's threshold reads as *maintenance*, perhaps the last working funerals in the Nexus. Fed to the sleeper, the restless, or a thing wearing the Dark One's name: referee's call. "Human" carries the teller's contempt; the purpose carries what they never ask.

**6.** *(Capability.)* A character reads **New Thracian** --- once outright, then on a Thought test. Ancient Thracian stays near-shut, one word in ten. Glyph-code of the old nodes refuses the grant, yielding only at a fed lararium under a Lorekeeper-aspect. Whoever drew it now holds every potsherd the party finds.

**7.** *Beware the God of Death.* *Beware*, never "active" --- and one reed-hush drunk spoke the theology cold: *he sleeps, he walks.* Both hold. The god dreams in a wound below, sealed by a rite that never finished; the Incarnation patrolling above reads as that dream given watch-duty, killing beast-kin and human alike. Beware the patrol, beware the waking, beware that the sleeper may hold the only true account of the fall --- and that dreaming gods price their answers.

**8.** (F) *The Caverns' rope bridges rotted through; crossings stay unsafe.* Phrasing reads decades stale. A span rotted once, word walked to the lake-towns, the world moved behind it --- recent traffic means at least one bridge stands new. A timestamp, not a liar. Trust it and burn rope and turns rigging a sound span, the wandering clock ticking each one; reach fresh hemp instead and learn *someone maintains this, recently, well* --- better intel than most truths here.

**9.** *"For safety, seek ye the Pool of Watery Wonders."* Archaic phrasing fossilizes an old order. The pool sits at the bottom, in the beast-king's palace --- four workings (summon, locate, scry, transit) through a chained sequence that reads as an Imperial facility-console, teleport drift the mark of 489 years without a calibration daemon. "For safety" once meant *the way out*: the gentry's evacuation route, retold till the context wore off. True three ways --- it grants escape, demands the full descent to reach, and turns into the king's longest arm the moment intruders are known.

**10.** *The Hall of Laughing Skulls leads somewhere; the skulls keep laughing.* Reads as a legacy access-corridor: skulls at lock-height, a two-point press with anti-tamper bite, a chuckle on every touch. Laughter as a stuck acknowledgment-chime, centuries past maintenance; bite as the anti-tamper still sampling hands. Beyond waits a live examiner in golden light, granting passage on judgment for wit or tribute. The rumor keeps the riches, drops the exam --- and the omission does the killing.

**11.** *Be wary of statuary.* The Iron Curse rotted iron in weeks; marble and bronze took none --- so where iron automatons seized and died, stone and bronze guardians held their footing, defaulting after five centuries to their oldest order: *an intruder is an intruder*. Wariness of every carved figure taxes torch and turn till complacency meets the one that walks. A bound guardian sometimes answers re-addressing over destruction --- right password, right authority, right offering. The watchmen never heard they were dismissed; nothing says they cannot be re-hired.

**12.** *A sunlit garden and a sparkling palace, deep under earth; something feeds it mana.* Form matches pre-revolt records; present state uncertain. A false-sun still burning under a built cloud-layer reads as a major standing draw that never went dark --- turning marvel into clue: *what feeds it* points at the lost core, a ley-tap, or the wounded node holding the god. Beauty and wound, one stone --- the garden *is* the pleasure-palace the enslaved carved, tended now by captive fae whose dead heat the boilers. Eden to the teller; five layered centuries of bondage to the history.

**13.** *A half-fae hermit may dwell near the ruins --- useful; spoken of as past more than present.* A threshold-and-road wanderer could persist by belonging to no faction, keeping guest-rite where empires don't reach, perhaps near an old Way. Aid runs real --- a hearth where the wandering clock stops, lore of which thresholds still open, uncorrupted memory of the *why* the Nexus forgot. Cost runs real too: hospitality given is hospitality owed, an errand, a carried sigil, faerie-kindness with a price. Living, or 200 years stale and pointing at a successor, a ghost, an empty burrow --- referee's call. The table needs one knock answered kind.

**14.** *Buried with a dead king: a powerful, evil sword that never rusted.* A blade whole through the Curse names itself *Named* --- iron storied deeply enough to slip the rot --- and that naming never reads neutral. "Evil" points at the deed it stays storied for, still running; the wielder inherits the bargain that kept the edge. *Which* king triangulates the campaign: the last Imperial king dead in his garden, the deathless reptile-king sharing the tomb and still moving, or a beast-dynasty liberator whose grave the word slanders. Each telling inflates the prize and strips the cost; the grave keeps both.

**15.** (F) *The Caverns go no deeper than two levels; only dark below.* Matches exactly how far the hill-folk descend --- courage's edge mistaken for the world's, built wholly from honest reports. Below the false floor: garden, palace, console-pool, the king's sword, the dreaming god --- so one lie conceals nearly every true prize. Trust it, provision for two levels, run out of light and clock three down. Dealt beside the garden-rumor, the contradiction works the seam: *a sunlit garden deep under earth, yet no deeper than two?*

**16.** (F) *The beast-king hoards immense wealth --- perhaps vast working magitech.* Same imperial houses, same slant. Reframes a free kingdom as a thieves' den, licensing plunder, erasing that the "wealth" was built by the beast-kin's own enslaved dead. The deep level holds *power*, not a hoard --- console, garden, boiler-works, sacred objects, a population. The lethal misread drives a party *toward* the throne-room when the thing that pays runs the other way: parley, with a court that has feared returning plunderers five centuries and will give a warband the welcome that fear earned.

**17.** *A pre-human remnant endures deep --- older than Thracia, older than the tomb-builders; it speaks through dreams.* The reptile-folk held the karst before the first humans, dwindled to the lizard-men, left a deathless sorcerer-king entombed while his kin guard the Shrine of the Ancient Ones. He carries the one memory reaching behind the manufactured word "human" --- the braid-truth as plain fact, logged before anyone buried it --- and perhaps the only account of who cut the unnatural deep cavern the golden age built upon. A lich amid lichdom in an Age of Undeath invites the fear of a tie to the trapped Imperator, where almost none runs: the reptile-king predates the whole scheme. What he knows prices like a campaign.

**18.** *The Beast Folk enslave valley-folk, worked in dark and steam.* True --- the briefing's hardest. Captives run as fae-folk lineages (though here that line means less than the teller thinks), held in the boiler-works that light the garden and heat the pool. A kingdom founded by the enslaved, five centuries on, reproduces bondage --- the same machine, new operator. Imperial houses tell it with satisfaction to launder their own; beast-kin dissidents tell it with grief against their court --- one true sentence, opposite mouths. The fact holds regardless. Hold two things at once: rumors 2 and 16 stay false, *and* this kingdom does a present wrong.

**19.** (F) *A good heart lays the restless dead to rest.* Imported with Dominion and Free-Cities newcomers; may hold where soul-roads still run, kills the faithful here. Undeath in this Nexus reads as a wounded Pattern, not a stain virtue scours --- Death fell silent, passage-rites broke, the dead cannot move on because *infrastructure* failed, not because evil won. What moves them runs on correct practice --- right rite, right name, right offering, right node --- orthopraxy, not a kind heart. The earnest one who carries this into the death-strata pays the grave's bill; yet learn the working rite and a good character becomes more than a banisher --- a ferryman.

**20.** *(Capability.)* A character speaks **New Thracian** as the hill-tribes hold it --- clear on a Thought test, garbled to the Singing Caryatids' tables on a miss. A New Delos phrasebook carries *pre-Maelstrom* speech; the deep clans speak 489 years of drift, so outsider-learned words may draw a penalty for sounding antique --- the mark of the wrong teacher. Clear speech opens the cheapest route to pinning the location and the only living human account of the deep. Speech grants the *possibility* of the talk; trust still answers to deeds and offerings.
