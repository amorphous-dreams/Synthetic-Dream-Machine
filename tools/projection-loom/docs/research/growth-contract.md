# From nameless relations to constructive operations

A location contains `Element` hypotheses in an **epistemic** graph. A typed
rewrite may spawn a proposed element, connect an authored relation, refine a
proposed capability, or explicitly release a proposal. Rewriting never changes
source-attested entities or links. The transaction clones a location snapshot,
checks dangling relations and containment cycles, then returns a new snapshot
with before/after digests and a causal receipt.

A *construction grammar* makes an independent, testable claim: a program of
`root-box`, attached `box`, `beam` and `void-box` operations produces fresh
CadQuery solids from bounded parameter expressions. A numerical threshold may
turn a whole branch on/off, and later operations may refer to earlier symbolic
keys. Anonymous branch handles may obtain stable construction slots, but are
not pet-names. A slot provides a relation for later reconciliation, not
certification of geometry.

A new rule may propose an arch. Only the real `SolidAssembly.audit(join=True)`
can promote the arch to an accepted CAD construction; all positive, negative
and traversal tests stay mandatory. Separate scene bodies do not need to fuse
into one global solid. Graph removal and pet-name o-takiage are **different**:
removal changes a *proposed hypothesis*, while o-takiage relaxes an identity
preference without removing its body, evidence or history.

The grammar intentionally omits learned macro synthesis, physical load tests,
IFC import and arbitrary mesh-to-CAD inversion. The research archive carries
ideas for those possible future operators, not a claim of implementation.
